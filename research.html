German Genealogy Sources & Research Status

This page lists the German archives and databases that should hold the records needed to fill in this family's history, plus an honest accounting of what we currently know and what we still need.

German Genealogy Sources

Archion

Protestant church records covering Germany. Most German Protestants for several centuries are documented here — baptisms, marriages, and burials drawn from the Evangelical regional church archives. Subscription-based, but the breadth of coverage is unmatched for Lutheran and Reformed families. archion.de

Matricula Online

Catholic church records for German-speaking regions. Free access to digitised parish registers from Catholic dioceses across Germany, Austria, and parts of central Europe — particularly strong for Bavaria and Austria. matricula-online.eu

FamilySearch

Free, large German records collection. Civil registers, parish records, indexed and browsable. Coverage is uneven but the price (free) and the cross-referencing tools make it the natural starting point. FamilySearch.org

Ancestry.de

German civil records, military records, and address books. The German edition of Ancestry holds collections that don't appear on the international version — address books (Adressbücher), military lists, and regional civil indexes. Ancestry.de

Bundesarchiv

The German federal archives. Particularly important for East German records. Since 2021, the Stasi-Unterlagen-Archiv — the Stasi Records Archive — has been part of the Bundesarchiv. This is where to request files held on East German citizens by the Ministry for State Security, including, where applicable, files relating to escape attempts. bundesarchiv.de

Standesamt (local civil registry offices)

Primary source for German vital records after 1876. Birth, marriage, and death certificates are issued and held by the Standesamt of the municipality where the event took place. After statutory waiting periods, certified copies can be ordered by descendants. For East German records, the receiving Standesamt for a given city today usually holds the GDR-era books.

Berlin Wall Memorial / Stiftung Berliner Mauer

Documentation of escapes from East Germany. The Stiftung holds and curates archival material on the inner-German border, the Berlin Wall, and the people who crossed it — including escape narratives, victim biographies, and contextual records. Useful both for documenting individual escapes and for the broader historical context. stiftung-berliner-mauer.de

Research Status

What we know

  • The family surname is Kohlmann, a German occupational name meaning “coal man” / charcoal burner.
  • Alvin Kohlmann — deceased — was Thomas Kohlmann's father.
  • Elise Christa Kohlmann (née Eifler), called Christa, was Thomas's mother. Born 25 November 1928 in Bautzen, married Alvin 24 July 1952 in Bautzen, died 21 May 2006 in Starnberg of cancer. Source: 2007 Eifler/Becker Ahnentafel (page 1).
  • Christa's older sister was Sigrid Michaelis (née Eifler), b. 20 July 1927 in Bautzen, married a Michaelis 20 August 1953 in Bautzen.
  • Christa's parents were Ernst Alwin Eifler (1892–1968), sole proprietor (Inhaber) of Stahldrahtmatratzen-Fabrik Weickert & Eifler in Bautzen, and Gertrud Elise Eifler, née Becker (1905–1973), born in Dresden, who worked alongside Alwin in the firm's office. The family's ownership of the firm and Gertrud's bookkeeping role are documented in two surviving company papers: an undated advertising letterhead from the Nazi period (1933–1945) and a business invoice dated 9 April 1943 with a payment-receipt note signed by Gertrud.
  • Gertrud Becker's parents were Richard Hermann Becker (1875–1938), a Kaufmann in Dresden, and Elise Becker, née Just (1880–1954), one of eight Just siblings.
  • Thomas Kohlmann was born in 1956 in Radebeul, Saxony (East Germany). He has a brother Matthias and a sister Sabine.
  • Rita Kohlmann was born in 1956 in Döbeln, Saxony (East Germany). Her birth surname was Woltmann (her mother's surname; her parents were not married at the time of her birth) and was later changed to Werner (her father's surname). Werner is the maiden name she carried into marriage.
  • Rita's mother is Brigitte Woltmann. Rita's father's surname is Werner; first name still to be confirmed.
  • Rita has two sisters: Heide and Annette.
  • Thomas escaped from East Germany to West Germany before 1980; he later helped Rita escape as well. They did not escape together.
  • Thomas and Rita married in Munich in 1980.
  • Their daughters are Valeria Kohlmann (b. 27 June 1990, Munich) and Elisa Kohlmann (b. 25 March 1994, Munich).
  • Valeria married Sebastian Belfiore in September 2022 in Munich; their son Enzo Belfiore was born in January 2026 in Tampa, Florida, and holds US, UK, and German citizenship.

What we need

  • Alvin Kohlmann's full birth and death dates and locations, his occupation, and his siblings if any.
  • Thomas's and Rita's exact birth dates, and the city in Germany where they live now.
  • Rita's father's first name, plus his and Brigitte's birth/death dates, locations, and whether they ever married.
  • Birth years and current locations for Matthias, Sabine, Heide, Annette, Elisa, and Sigrid; maiden surnames for Heide and Annette (Woltmann or Werner, depending on birth year relative to Rita's name change); current status (living/deceased) for Sigrid.
  • The specifics of Thomas's escape — date, route, who helped, where he first landed in West Germany.
  • The specifics of Rita's escape — date, route, the plan Thomas put together, anyone else involved.
  • The Kohlmann (paternal) deeper ancestral origin: where the Kohlmann line traces back to before Saxony, and how long it had been in Radebeul / Saxony before Thomas's birth in 1956. The Bautzen civil marriage record from 24 July 1952 (Alvin Kohlmann × Christa Eifler) would name Alvin's parents, birthplace, and occupation, and is the cleanest single source to chase.
  • Identity of the founding partner Weickert in Weickert & Eifler, and the circumstances of his exit (death, retirement, or buyout) by the 1930s. Likely sources: Bautzen Handelsregister entries, Stadtarchiv Bautzen, regional trade directories.
  • Founding date of Weickert & Eifler, and the post-1945 fate of the firm under Soviet occupation and the GDR.
  • Page 2 of the 2007 Eifler/Becker Ahnentafel, which is expected to extend the Eifler line one further generation back and possibly the Just line as well.
  • Whether Stasi files exist for Thomas, Rita, or anyone close to them, and if so what they contain.

Open Research Threads

✓ Confirmed: Weickert & Eifler ownership

Ernst Alwin Eifler (1892–1968), Thomas Kohlmann's maternal grandfather, is documented as sole proprietor (Inhaber) of the firm in two surviving company documents: an undated Nazi-period letterhead and a business invoice dated 9 April 1943 (the latter with a payment-receipt note signed by his wife Gertrud Eifler). The firm's identification with the family is no longer hypothesis.

The Weickert exit

The firm preserved the founder Weickert's name long after he was no longer the active partner. By the 1930s Alwin Eifler was sole proprietor. Weickert's full identity, dates, and the circumstances of his departure (death, retirement, or buyout) are unknown. Likely sources: Bautzen Handelsregister entries, Stadtarchiv Bautzen, regional trade directories.

Founding date of the firm

Not yet documented. The Reich patent number 435041 on the “Elite” mattress, if independently traceable in the Deutsches Patent- und Markenamt archive, would give a hard upper bound for at least that product line.

The factory at Singwitz

Industriegelände Singwitz, south of Bautzen, with its own rail siding via Gnaschwitz. Whether the factory survived the war, what happened to it under Soviet occupation and the GDR, and whether the building still stands in some form are open questions.

Post-war fate of the firm

Private manufacturing concerns in the Soviet zone and the later GDR were systematically expropriated through the late 1940s and the 1972 nationalisation wave. Alwin Eifler lived until 1968 in Bautzen. What his relationship was to whatever became of the firm after 1945 is not documented. Likely sources: Sächsische Zeitung archive, Bautzen Adressbücher 1945–1968, Stadtarchiv Bautzen.

Page 2 of the 2007 Ahnentafel

The Ahnentafel we have is page 1 of at least 2. Page 2 is indicated by a handwritten ↓ Seite 2 in the lower-right corner of page 1 but has not yet been digitised. It will likely extend the Eifler line one further generation back, and possibly the Just line as well.

Sigrid Michaelis (née Eifler)

Christa's older sister, born 20 July 1927 in Bautzen, married a Michaelis on 20 August 1953 in Bautzen. Husband's first name and her death date (if deceased) are not known. She would be 98 in 2025; if living, she or her descendants would be one of the most direct living sources for memory of the Eifler firm and of Bautzen under the GDR.

Marie [Wettlitz?] of Rothermundtstraße 5, Dresden

A handwritten note on the Ahnentafel names Marie, sister of Richard Hermann Becker (b. 1875). Married surname uncertain: possibilities include Wettlitz, Wettlich, or Wettnitz. The Dresden address is in Striesen and is verifiable. The Dresdner Adressbücher (digitised by SLUB Dresden) for residents at that address in the 1920s–1940s should be checked.

The 1968 car accident

Alwin Eifler died of a basilar skull fracture in a car accident on 18 December 1968 in Bautzen. The Sächsische Zeitung, Bautzen edition, would likely have reported this. Worth pulling.

Alwin Eifler's siblings: Max, Alfred, Martha

Names known; dates and descendants unknown. Any who stayed in Sohland an der Spree or Bautzen may have descendants today.

Cancer cluster on the maternal line

Both Christa (d. 2006) and her mother Gertrud Becker Eifler (d. 1973) died of cancer. Two data points is not a pattern but worth recording for any future medical-history conversation with Thomas, Matthias, and Sabine.

Alvin Kohlmann

Thomas's paternal grandfather is still entirely a placeholder. The Bautzen civil marriage record from 24 July 1952 (Alvin Kohlmann × Elise Christa Eifler) would name his parents, birthplace, and occupation, and is the cleanest single source to chase.

Primary Sources

Family Ahnentafel, 18 January 2007, page 1

Typed German genealogy chart compiled by an unidentified family member, almost certainly in the months following the death of Elise Christa Kohlmann (21 May 2006). Covers the Eifler / Becker maternal line over three generations. Two pages; page 2 not yet digitised. Family collection.

Weickert & Eifler advertising letterhead

Undated, Nazi period (1933–1945), most likely late 1930s. Pre-printed promotional card / postcard in Fraktur typography, illustrated with the “Elite” mattress and its Reich patent number, naming Alwin Eifler as proprietor. Carries a blank line for Überreicht durch: (“Presented by:”), meaning it was a presentation card given out by company representatives. Family collection.

Weickert & Eifler business invoice, 9 April 1943

Issued to Tischlermeister Erich Lehmann, Klix Nr. 58d, Kreis Bautzen, for one upholstered desk-chair seat at 5,50 Reichsmark plus handling. Bears a handwritten payment-receipt acknowledgment dated 28 April 1943, signed in ink by Gertrud Eifler. Carries the firm's Reichs-Betriebs-Nr. stamped in blue. Family collection.
