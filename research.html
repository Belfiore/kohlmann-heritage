German Genealogy Sources & Research Status

This page lists the German archives and databases that should hold the records needed to fill in this family's history, plus an honest accounting of what we currently know and what we still need.

German Genealogy Sources

Archion

Protestant church records covering Germany. Most German Protestants for several centuries are documented here, baptisms, marriages, and burials drawn from the Evangelical regional church archives. Subscription-based, but the breadth of coverage is unmatched for Lutheran and Reformed families. archion.de

Matricula Online

Catholic church records for German-speaking regions. Free access to digitised parish registers from Catholic dioceses across Germany, Austria, and parts of central Europe, particularly strong for Bavaria and Austria. matricula-online.eu

FamilySearch

Free, large German records collection. Civil registers, parish records, indexed and browsable. Coverage is uneven but the price (free) and the cross-referencing tools make it the natural starting point. FamilySearch.org

Ancestry.de

German civil records, military records, and address books. The German edition of Ancestry holds collections that don't appear on the international version, address books (Adressbücher), military lists, and regional civil indexes. Ancestry.de

Bundesarchiv

The German federal archives. Particularly important for East German records. Since 2021, the Stasi-Unterlagen-Archiv, the Stasi Records Archive, has been part of the Bundesarchiv. This is where to request files held on East German citizens by the Ministry for State Security, including, where applicable, files relating to escape attempts. bundesarchiv.de

Standesamt (local civil registry offices)

Primary source for German vital records after 1876. Birth, marriage, and death certificates are issued and held by the Standesamt of the municipality where the event took place. After statutory waiting periods, certified copies can be ordered by descendants. For East German records, the receiving Standesamt for a given city today usually holds the GDR-era books.

Berlin Wall Memorial / Stiftung Berliner Mauer

Documentation of escapes from East Germany. The Stiftung holds and curates archival material on the inner-German border, the Berlin Wall, and the people who crossed it, including escape narratives, victim biographies, and contextual records. Useful both for documenting individual escapes and for the broader historical context. stiftung-berliner-mauer.de

Research Status

What we know

  • The family surname is Kohlmann, a German occupational name meaning “coal man” / charcoal burner.
  • Herbert Kohlmann (Herbert Coleman, as the family says it in English), deceased, was Thomas Kohlmann's father. Earlier versions of this site gave his first name wrongly, mixing him up with Christa's father, Alwin Eifler; the family corrected it on 26 November 2026.
  • Elise Christa Kohlmann (née Eifler), called Christa, was Thomas's mother. Born 25 November 1928 in Bautzen, married Herbert 24 July 1952 in Bautzen, died 21 May 2006 in Starnberg of cancer. Source: 2007 Eifler/Becker Ahnentafel (page 1).
  • Christa's older sister was Sigrid Michaelis (née Eifler), b. 20 July 1927 in Bautzen, married a Michaelis 20 August 1953 in Bautzen.
  • Christa's parents were Ernst Alwin Eifler (1892 to 1968), sole proprietor (Inhaber) of Stahldrahtmatratzen-Fabrik Weickert & Eifler in Bautzen, and Gertrud Elise Eifler, née Becker (1905 to 1973), born in Dresden, who worked alongside Alwin in the firm's office. The family's ownership of the firm and Gertrud's bookkeeping role are documented in two surviving company papers: an undated advertising letterhead from the Nazi period (1933 to 1945) and a business invoice dated 9 April 1943 with a payment-receipt note signed by Gertrud.
  • Gertrud Becker's parents were Richard Hermann Becker (1875 to 1938), a Kaufmann in Dresden, and Elise Becker, née Just (1880 to 1954), one of eight Just siblings.
  • Thomas Kohlmann was born in 1956 in Radebeul, Saxony (East Germany). He has a brother Matthias and a sister Sabine.
  • Rita Kohlmann was born in 1956 in Döbeln, Saxony (East Germany). Her birth surname was Woltmann (her mother's surname; her parents were not married at the time of her birth) and was later changed to Werner (her father's surname). Werner is the maiden name she carried into marriage.
  • Rita's mother is Brigitte Woltmann. Rita's father's surname is Werner; first name still to be confirmed.
  • Rita has two sisters: Heide and Annette.
  • Thomas escaped from East Germany to West Germany before 1980; he later helped Rita escape as well. They did not escape together.
  • Thomas and Rita married in Munich in 1980.
  • Their daughters are Valeria Kohlmann (b. 27 June 1990, Munich) and Elisa Kohlmann (b. 25 March 1994, Munich).
  • Valeria married Sebastian Belfiore in September 2022 in Munich; their son Enzo Belfiore was born in January 2026 in Tampa, Florida, and holds US, UK, and German citizenship.

What we need

  • Herbert Kohlmann's full birth and death dates and locations, his occupation, his parents, and his siblings if any.
  • Thomas's and Rita's exact birth dates, and the city in Germany where they live now.
  • Rita's father's first name, plus his and Brigitte's birth/death dates, locations, and whether they ever married.
  • Birth years and current locations for Matthias, Sabine, Heide, Annette, Elisa, and Sigrid; maiden surnames for Heide and Annette (Woltmann or Werner, depending on birth year relative to Rita's name change); current status (living/deceased) for Sigrid.
  • The specifics of Thomas's escape: date, route, who helped, where he first landed in West Germany.
  • The specifics of Rita's escape: date, route, the plan Thomas put together, anyone else involved.
  • The Kohlmann (paternal) deeper ancestral origin: where the Kohlmann line traces back to before Saxony, and how long it had been in Radebeul / Saxony before Thomas's birth in 1956. The Bautzen civil marriage record from 24 July 1952 (Herbert Kohlmann × Christa Eifler) would name Herbert's parents, birthplace, and occupation, and is the cleanest single source to chase.
  • Identity of the founding partner Weickert in Weickert & Eifler, and the circumstances of his exit (death, retirement, or buyout) by the 1930s. Likely sources: Bautzen Handelsregister entries, Stadtarchiv Bautzen, regional trade directories.
  • Founding date of Weickert & Eifler, and the post-1945 fate of the firm under Soviet occupation and the GDR.
  • Page 2 of the 2007 Eifler/Becker Ahnentafel, which is expected to extend the Eifler line one further generation back and possibly the Just line as well.
  • Whether Stasi files exist for Thomas, Rita, or anyone close to them, and if so what they contain.

Open Research Threads

✓ Confirmed: Weickert & Eifler ownership

Ernst Alwin Eifler (1892 to 1968), Thomas Kohlmann's maternal grandfather, is sole owner (Inhaber) of the firm in the Bautzen directories of 1928, 1929, 1932, 1935 and 1938 (commercial register Nr. 885, later A 173), as well as on the family's letterhead and the 9 April 1943 invoice. See the Weickert & Eifler page.

The Weickert exit

Found: the Weickert was Bruno Weickert, master saddler, who ran the firm's saddlery and upholstery shop at Löbauer Straße 10 in 1921 (Bautzen directory 1921, p. 121). By 1928 Alwin Eifler was sole owner. When and why Bruno left is still open; the commercial register file at the Staatsfilialarchiv Bautzen would show it.

Founding date of the firm

Between 1917 and 1921. No Eifler is in the 1917 Bautzen directory; the firm is in the 1921 directory. Not before the First World War, as the site used to say. Patent 435041 on the “Elite” mattress belongs to August Filter of Hannover (published 6 October 1926), so the letterhead dates after that.

The factory at Singwitz

Industriegelände Singwitz, south of Bautzen, with its own rail siding via Gnaschwitz. Whether the factory survived the war, what happened to it under Soviet occupation and the GDR, and whether the building still stands in some form are open questions.

Post-war fate of the firm

Private manufacturing concerns in the Soviet zone and the later GDR were systematically expropriated through the late 1940s and the 1972 nationalisation wave. Alwin Eifler lived until 1968 in Bautzen. What his relationship was to whatever became of the firm after 1945 is not documented. Likely sources: Sächsische Zeitung archive, Bautzen Adressbücher 1945 to 1968, Stadtarchiv Bautzen.

Page 2 of the 2007 Ahnentafel

The Ahnentafel we have is page 1 of at least 2. Page 2 is indicated by a handwritten ↓ Seite 2 in the lower-right corner of page 1 but has not yet been digitised. It will likely extend the Eifler line one further generation back, and possibly the Just line as well.

Sigrid Michaelis (née Eifler)

Christa's older sister, born 20 July 1927 in Bautzen, married a Michaelis on 20 August 1953 in Bautzen. Husband's first name and her death date (if deceased) are not known. She would be 98 in 2025; if living, she or her descendants would be one of the most direct living sources for memory of the Eifler firm and of Bautzen under the GDR.

Marie [Wettlitz?] of Rothermundtstraße 5, Dresden

A handwritten note on the Ahnentafel names Marie, sister of Richard Hermann Becker (b. 1875). Married surname uncertain: possibilities include Wettlitz, Wettlich, or Wettnitz. The Dresden address is in Striesen and is verifiable. The Dresdner Adressbücher (digitised by SLUB Dresden) for residents at that address in the 1920s to 1940s should be checked.

The 1968 car accident

The family chart says Alwin Eifler died of a basal skull fracture after a car accident on 18 December 1968, in Bautzen. His death is not in the Bautzen city death register for 1968. A note stamped on his 1926 Dresden marriage entry on 6 November 1969 explains why: the death was registered at Zittau, Nr. 1169/1968 (number as read; 1926 marriage entry, margin note (Ancestry)). The Sächsische Zeitung, Bautzen edition, may have reported it.

Alwin Eifler's siblings: Max, Alfred, Martha

Names known; dates and descendants unknown. Any who stayed in Sohland an der Spree or Bautzen may have descendants today.

Cancer cluster on the maternal line

Both Christa (d. 2006) and her mother Gertrud Becker Eifler (d. 1973) died of cancer. Two data points is not a pattern but worth recording for any future medical-history conversation with Thomas, Matthias, and Sabine.

Herbert Kohlmann

Thomas's father, Valeria's paternal grandfather, is still mostly a placeholder. The Bautzen civil marriage record from 24 July 1952 (Herbert Kohlmann × Elise Christa Eifler) would name his parents, birthplace, and occupation, and is the cleanest single source to chase. The 1943/44 Radebeul address book has only one Kohlmann, Dr. med. Johannes Kohlmann, a surgeon at Carolastraße 16, so Herbert's family was probably not living in Radebeul in 1943.

Lead: Alwin Kohlmann, butcher, Bautzen

The Bautzen address books list one Alwin Kohlmann every year from 1921 to 1938. In 1921 he is a journeyman butcher (Fleischergeselle) at Holzmarkt 27, in the rear building; in 1924 a butcher (Fleischer) at Holzmarkt 27, ground floor; and from 1925 to 1938 a butcher at Schäfferstraße 6. No entry was found for 1941 or 1943. Same surname and town as Herbert Kohlmann, Thomas's father. He was born in 1883, so he could belong to Herbert's parents' generation; whether he is related at all is not known. He is shown on the tree and the map as a suspected relative with no proven link. The Bautzen marriage name indexes for men are online at the Archivverbund Bautzen and are the next free place to look for his marriage. New: the Bautzen death name index for 1972 lists an Ernst Alwin Kohlmann who died on 27 February 1972 (register number 185). He is the only Alwin Kohlmann in the Bautzen death indexes from 1946 to 1979. Confirmed: his entry in the 1972 death register (Nr. 185) gives his address as Schäfferstraße 6, the butcher's house, so he is the same man. He was born on 21 September 1883 at Kötzschau near Lawalde (Kreis Löbau; birth registered at Lawalde, Nr. 71/1883) and was married to Marie Ida née Jurack (marriage registered at Malschwitz, Nr. 9/1914); she died on 20 January 1973. The entry names no parents or children. The 1914 marriage entry would name his parents, and the 1952 Bautzen marriage entry, which names Herbert's parents, would show whether this family is connected to ours at all. The 1980 death register (Nr. 40) adds a probable sister: Ernestine Hedwig Kissal née Kohlmann, born on 15 September 1897 at Kleindehsa, the village Kötzschau belongs to, married at Lawalde in 1926 to Ernst Hermann Kissal, died in Bautzen on 14 January 1980.

The innkeepers of Kleindehsa. The village chronicle of Kleindehsa says that in 1787 Gottfried Kohlmann, son of the village judge (Richter) Johann Kohlmann of Kötzschau, bought the village inn with its farm (the Kretscham) for 600 Taler, and that the inn stayed with the Kohlmanns for about 150 years: Johann Traugott Kohlmann from 1809, Ernst Kohlmann from 1860, his widow from 1888, and Karl August Reinhold Kohlmann, who took over the inn in 1885. A hall was opened on its south side in 1927. Later Gustav Kohlmann owned it, and after his death his brother Ernst and Ernst’s daughter ran it until 1959. In the GDR it became a holiday camp of the lignite combine VEB Braunkohlenkombinat Senftenberg. It burned down on the evening of 29 January 1997 and was pulled down. [Lawalde: Chronik Kleindehsa] The chronicle gives no sources. The butcher Ernst Alwin Kohlmann was born in Kötzschau in 1883 and his probable sister in Kleindehsa in 1897, so he very likely belongs to this family of village judges and innkeepers. That is not yet proven: his 1883 Lawalde birth entry (Nr. 71) would name his parents.

More Kohlmanns of Kleindehsa in other researchers’ trees. Two Ancestry member trees, neither citing a source, add names. The “Kirchhoff Family” tree has Maria Alwine Kohlmann, born at Kleindehsa on 23 May 1868, daughter of Karl Friedrich Ernst Kohlmann and Christiane Elisabeth Kriegel (born 1848); she married Fritz Kirchhoff at Lawalde in 1891 and died at Kleindehsa in 1900. Her father may be the innkeeper Ernst Kohlmann of 1860, whose widow held the inn in 1888. Her names, Alwine and Ernst, echo the butcher Ernst Alwin Kohlmann, born at nearby Kötzschau in 1883, but if the innkeeper died before 1888 he cannot be the father of Ernestine Hedwig, born 1897, so the pieces do not all fit yet. The “Augsten” tree has Johanne Sophie Helene Kohlmann of Kleindehsa, who married Johann Carl Traugott Kutschke at Cunnewalde; their son, born in 1857, was named Karl Friedrich Ernst. These are leads for the Lawalde church books, not records. They sit in the Kleindehsa group on the tree, apart from the family.

A possible Sorbian great-grandmother, not yet proven to be family. The butcher’s wife, Marie Ida née Jurack, was born in 1887 in Briesing near Malschwitz. When the Sorbian scholar Arnošt Muka counted the villages in 1884 and 1885, 176 of Briesing’s 183 people were Sorbs, and Jurack is a Sorbian surname (from Jurij, George). So she almost certainly grew up in a Sorbian-speaking village; whether her own family spoke Sorbian is not yet known. In May 1813 the allied armies held Briesing against Napoleon in the Battle of Bautzen. [Wikipedia: Briesing]

Other Kohlmanns in Bautzen (leads only)

Other Kohlmanns in the Bautzen address books, with no known link to Herbert: Walter Kohlmann, a state trade inspector (Regierungsgewerberat, in 1935 also Diplom-Ingenieur), Taucherstraße 13 (1928 to 1935); Friedrich Kohlmann, merchant, Jägerstraße 43 (1935, 1938); Heinz Kohlmann, Prokurist, Kaiserstraße 12 (1935, 1938), full names Karl Heinz, who married Ilse Mader in Bautzen in 1935 (Nr. 32/1935); her entry in the 1980 death register (Nr. 942) gives her birth as 23 July 1909 in Bautzen and shows Karl Heinz Kohlmann still alive in November 1980; and Willy Kohlmann, merchant, Kantstraße 21 (1935). The 1924 book for the villages of the Bautzen district also lists a Magdalene, a Karl, an Oskar and a Moritz Kohlmann. None of them has been tied to the family. The Bautzen death name indexes add more Kohlmanns, again with no known link: Anna Ida née Schröder (1953, wife of Willy), Karl Martin of Crostau (24 January 1963, perhaps the Crostau locksmith of 1924), Karl Paul (1969), Max Oskar (1970), and in 1971 an Auguste Lina and a Selmar Ferdinand Erich whose surname may read Kollmann. No Eifler death appears in the Bautzen city indexes from 1946 to 1972, including 1968, and Ernst Alwin Eifler's death, it turns out, was registered at Zittau. Gertrud Eifler is in the 1973 index as expected. The 1974 and 1975 indexes also list a Martha Eifler née König, an Elsbeth Brade née Eifler and a Rosalie Eifler; how they connect to the family is not known yet. The 1978 register (Nr. 704) shows that the Max Eifler of Paulistraße 32 in the 1928 and 1938 address books was Max Oswald Eifler, a railway platform conductor, born on 2 March 1892 at Oberrennersdorf near Herrnhut and married to Lina Ella née Ullrich; he died on 23 September 1978. He was born only seven months before Alwin Eifler (7 October 1892, Sohland), so he was not Alwin's brother Max; perhaps a cousin. Alwin's brother Max is still to be found. The Ancestry address books show him at Paulistraße 32, third floor, as an assistant platform conductor (Hilfs-Bahnsteigschaffner) in every Bautzen book from 1921 to 1935; how he was related to Alwin, if at all, is still a lead. The 1979 index lists a Liesbeth Eifler.

Lead: Max Woltmann, Döbeln

Max Woltmann, a tax official, is the only Woltmann in the Döbeln address books of 1925 (Steuerassistent, Kleinbauchlitzer Straße 20) and 1939 (Steuersekretär, Grimmaische Straße 20). The 1939 book says Grimmaische Straße was formerly Kleinbauchlitzer Straße, so it is the same house. There was no Woltmann in Döbeln in 1900-01. With so rare a name he may be Brigitte Woltmann's father or grandfather. Not proven: Brigitte's birth entry would settle it.

Lead: Ernst Eifler of Sohland

Ernst Eifler, a farmer of Sohland an der Spree Nr. 247, aged 61, was a witness at Alwin and Gertrud's wedding in Dresden in 1926. He is probably Alwin's father. Alwin's 1892 Sohland birth or baptism entry would prove it and name his mother.

Primary Sources

Family Ahnentafel, 18 January 2007, page 1

Typed German genealogy chart compiled by an unidentified family member, almost certainly in the months following the death of Elise Christa Kohlmann (21 May 2006). Covers the Eifler / Becker maternal line over three generations. Two pages; page 2 not yet digitised. Family collection.

Weickert & Eifler advertising letterhead

Undated, Nazi period (1933 to 1945), most likely late 1930s. Pre-printed promotional card / postcard in Fraktur typography, illustrated with the “Elite” mattress and its Reich patent number, naming Alwin Eifler as proprietor. Carries a blank line for Überreicht durch: (“Presented by:”), meaning it was a presentation card given out by company representatives. Family collection.

Weickert & Eifler business invoice, 9 April 1943

Issued to Tischlermeister Erich Lehmann, Klix Nr. 58d, Kreis Bautzen, for one upholstered desk-chair seat at 5,50 Reichsmark plus handling. Bears a handwritten payment-receipt acknowledgment dated 28 April 1943, signed in ink by Gertrud Eifler. Carries the firm's Reichs-Betriebs-Nr. stamped in blue. Family collection.

Bautzen Adressbuch 1938, page 14

Identified: page 14 of the 1938 Bautzen Adressbuch (SLUB Dresden digital copy, image 56). The highlighted line reads “Eifler, Alwin, Kaufmann, Martin-Mutschmann-Str. 19, Fernspr. 2512”. It is about Christa's father Alwin Eifler, not Thomas's father. Family collection.
