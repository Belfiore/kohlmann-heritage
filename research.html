German Genealogy Sources & Research Status

This page lists the German archives and databases that should hold the records needed to fill in this family's history, plus an honest accounting of what we currently know and what we still need.

German Genealogy Sources

Archion

Protestant church records covering Germany. Most German Protestants for several centuries are documented here — baptisms, marriages, and burials drawn from the Evangelical regional church archives. Subscription-based, but the breadth of coverage is unmatched for Lutheran and Reformed families. archion.de

Matricula Online

Catholic church records for German-speaking regions. Free access to digitised parish registers from Catholic dioceses across Germany, Austria, and parts of central Europe — particularly strong for Bavaria and Austria. matricula-online.eu

FamilySearch

Free, large German records collection. Civil registers, parish records, indexed and browsable. Coverage is uneven but the price (free) and the cross-referencing tools make it the natural starting point. FamilySearch.org

Ancestry.de

German civil records, military records, and address books. The German edition of Ancestry holds collections that don't appear on the international version — address books (Adressbücher), military lists, and regional civil indexes. Ancestry.de

Bundesarchiv

The German federal archives. Particularly important for East German records. Since 2021, the Stasi-Unterlagen-Archiv — the Stasi Records Archive — has been part of the Bundesarchiv. This is where to request files held on East German citizens by the Ministry for State Security, including, where applicable, files relating to escape attempts. bundesarchiv.de

Standesamt (local civil registry offices)

Primary source for German vital records after 1876. Birth, marriage, and death certificates are issued and held by the Standesamt of the municipality where the event took place. After statutory waiting periods, certified copies can be ordered by descendants. For East German records, the receiving Standesamt for a given city today usually holds the GDR-era books.

Berlin Wall Memorial / Stiftung Berliner Mauer

Documentation of escapes from East Germany. The Stiftung holds and curates archival material on the inner-German border, the Berlin Wall, and the people who crossed it — including escape narratives, victim biographies, and contextual records. Useful both for documenting individual escapes and for the broader historical context. stiftung-berliner-mauer.de

Research Status

What we know

  • The family surname is Kohlmann, a German occupational name meaning “coal man” / charcoal burner.
  • Alvin Kohlmann — deceased — was Thomas Kohlmann's father.
  • Elise Christa Kohlmann (née Eifler), called Christa, was Thomas's mother. Born 25 November 1928 in Bautzen, married Alvin 24 July 1952 in Bautzen, died 21 May 2006 in Starnberg of cancer. Source: 2007 Eifler/Becker Ahnentafel (page 1).
  • Christa's older sister was Sigrid Michaelis (née Eifler), b. 20 July 1927 in Bautzen, married a Michaelis 20 August 1953 in Bautzen.
  • Christa's parents were Ernst Alwin Eifler (1892–1968), a Fabrikant in Bautzen, and Gertrud Elise Eifler, née Becker (1905–1973), born in Dresden. Ernst Alwin was almost certainly the “Eifler” in Weickert & Eifler — high probability, but not yet documentarily confirmed.
  • Gertrud Becker's parents were Richard Hermann Becker (1875–1938), a Kaufmann in Dresden, and Elise Becker, née Just (1880–1954), one of eight Just siblings.
  • Thomas Kohlmann was born in 1956 in Radebeul, Saxony (East Germany). He has a brother Matthias and a sister Sabine.
  • Rita Kohlmann was born in 1956 in Döbeln, Saxony (East Germany). Her birth surname was Woltmann (her mother's surname; her parents were not married at the time of her birth) and was later changed to Werner (her father's surname). Werner is the maiden name she carried into marriage.
  • Rita's mother is Brigitte Woltmann. Rita's father's surname is Werner; first name still to be confirmed.
  • Rita has two sisters: Heide and Annette.
  • Thomas escaped from East Germany to West Germany before 1980; he later helped Rita escape as well. They did not escape together.
  • Thomas and Rita married in Munich in 1980.
  • Their daughters are Valeria Kohlmann (b. 27 June 1990, Munich) and Elisa Kohlmann (b. 25 March 1994, Munich).
  • Valeria married Sebastian Belfiore in September 2022 in Munich; their son Enzo Belfiore was born in January 2026 in Tampa, Florida, and holds US, UK, and German citizenship.

What we need

  • Alvin Kohlmann's full birth and death dates and locations, his occupation, and his siblings if any.
  • Thomas's and Rita's exact birth dates, and the city in Germany where they live now.
  • Rita's father's first name, plus his and Brigitte's birth/death dates, locations, and whether they ever married.
  • Birth years and current locations for Matthias, Sabine, Heide, Annette, Elisa, and Sigrid; maiden surnames for Heide and Annette (Woltmann or Werner, depending on birth year relative to Rita's name change); current status (living/deceased) for Sigrid.
  • The specifics of Thomas's escape — date, route, who helped, where he first landed in West Germany.
  • The specifics of Rita's escape — date, route, the plan Thomas put together, anyone else involved.
  • Documentary confirmation of Ernst Alwin Eifler's role in Weickert & Eifler — Handelsregister entry, company materials, or family testimony.
  • The Kohlmann (paternal) deeper ancestral origin — where the Kohlmann line traces back to before Saxony, and how long it had been in Radebeul / Saxony before Thomas's birth in 1956.
  • Page 2 of the 2007 Eifler/Becker Ahnentafel, which is expected to extend the Eifler line one further generation back and possibly the Just line as well.
  • Whether Stasi files exist for Thomas, Rita, or anyone close to them, and if so what they contain.

Open Research Threads

Confirm Weickert & Eifler ownership

Source needed: Handelsregister entry for Weickert & Eifler, held in the Sächsisches Staatsarchiv (Dresden or Leipzig holdings), which would name the firm's principals and so confirm or refute the working hypothesis that Ernst Alwin Eifler was its “Eifler.” Also: surviving company materials (letterheads, invoices, advertisements) and direct family testimony from Thomas, Matthias, and Sabine.

Page 2 of the 2007 Ahnentafel

The Ahnentafel we have is page 1 of at least 2. Page 2 is indicated by a handwritten ↓ Seite 2 in the lower-right corner of page 1 but has not yet been digitised. It will likely extend the Eifler line one further generation back (Ernst Alwin Eifler's parents) and possibly the Just line as well.

Sigrid Michaelis (née Eifler)

Christa's older sister, b. 20 July 1927 in Bautzen, married a Michaelis on 20 August 1953 in Bautzen. Husband's first name and her current status (living/deceased) are not known. She would be 98 in 2025; if living, she would be one of the oldest direct sources for family memory of the Eifler firm and of life in Bautzen through the GDR period.

Marie [Wettlitz?] of Rothermundtstraße 5, Dresden

A handwritten note on page 1 of the Ahnentafel names Marie, sister of Richard Hermann Becker (b. 1875). Married surname uncertain — possibilities include Wettlitz, Wettlich, or Wettnitz. The Dresden address is in Striesen and is verifiable. Worth checking the Dresdner Adressbücher (digitised by SLUB Dresden) for residents at that address in the 1920s–1940s.

The 1968 car accident

Ernst Alwin Eifler died of a basilar skull fracture (Schädelbasisbruch) in a car accident on 18 December 1968 in Bautzen. The Sächsische Zeitung, Bautzen edition, would likely have reported this. Worth pulling.

Ernst Alwin Eifler's siblings

The Ahnentafel records three siblings of Ernst Alwin: brothers Max and Alfred and sister Martha. Names are known; dates and descendants are not. If any of them stayed in Sohland or Bautzen, descendants may exist who would be distant cousins to Thomas.

Cancer cluster on the maternal line

Both Christa (d. 2006) and her mother Gertrud Becker Eifler (d. 1973) died of cancer (Krebs). Two data points is not a pattern, but worth noting in any conversation with Thomas about family medical history.
