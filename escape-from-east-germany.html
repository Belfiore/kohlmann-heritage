Historical Context

Between 1949 and the construction of the Berlin Wall in August 1961, roughly 2.5 to 3 million East Germans fled to the West — about a sixth of the GDR's entire population. Most crossed through Berlin, where the boundary between the Soviet and Western sectors remained relatively porous. The hemorrhage of working-age citizens was the proximate reason the SED government built the Wall.

After the Wall went up, escape became dramatically more dangerous. Between 1961 and the fall of the Wall in November 1989, an estimated 5,000 or more people successfully escaped over, under, or through the inner-German border and the Berlin Wall. At least 140 people died at the Berlin Wall itself, and many more — the exact number is still being established by historians — died at the inner-German border. (These figures are well-documented historical estimates from the Berlin Wall Memorial / Stiftung Berliner Mauer and the Bundesarchiv.)
