Fabrikant Bautzen, 1921 to 1943
Weickert & Eifler

Stahldrahtmatratzen-Fabrik, Bautzen

Eifler · Bautzen

Weickert & Eifler began as a small saddlery and upholstery shop in Bautzen and grew into a steel wire mattress factory in the Spree valley south of the town. This page tells its story from the records found so far. Each statement carries a label: FACTa record says it; PROBABLEthe records point to it; LEADa clue still to be tested; UNKNOWNnot yet found; CORRECTEDthe site used to say something different. The numbers in brackets link to the source.

A Saddler’s Shop, 1917 to 1921

CORRECTEDThe site used to say the firm was founded before the First World War. The records do not support that. The Bautzen directories of 1894, 1898, 1900, 1901 and 1917 list no Eifler at all [2], and Alwin Eifler was only 21 when the war began.

FACTThe earliest record of the firm is the Bautzen directory of 1921, page 121: “Weickert, Bruno, Sattlermeister (Fa.: Weickert & Eifler), Sattler- und Polstergeschäft, Löbauer Str. 10, Fernruf 512 (Wohnung: Neusalzaer Straße 34, II)” [1]. So in 1921 the firm was a master saddler’s saddlery and upholstery business at Löbauer Straße 10, with Bruno Weickert as the working craftsman. He lived at Neusalzaer Straße 34. The founding therefore falls between 1917 and 1921 PROBABLE.

LEADThe same 1921 page lists “Karl, Schuhmachermstr., Neusalzaer Str. 20, II”, a master shoemaker. Karl Gottlieb Weickert is already at Neusalzaer Straße 20 in 1917 [2], and Bruno is at the same house in 1928 [4], so Karl is very likely Bruno’s father. Not yet proven.

Upholstery is the link to what came later: the factory’s range still included upholstered toppers and upholstered furniture alongside its wire mattresses [9].

Alwin Eifler from Sohland

FACTErnst Alwin Eifler was born on 7 October 1892 in Sohland an der Spree, a village in the Upper Lusatian uplands south of Bautzen. His marriage entry gives the place and cites his birth entry in the Sohland register [3].

FACTOn 2 October 1926, at the Dresden V registry office (1926 Nr. 352), Alwin, described as a Fabrikant (manufacturer) living at Wendischer Graben 3, Bautzen, married Gertrud Elise Becker, 21, of Rothermundtstraße 5, Dresden. The witnesses were Ernst Eifler, a farmer (Landwirt) of Sohland an der Spree Nr. 247, aged 61, and the bride’s father, the merchant Richard Becker, 51 [3].

LEADErnst Eifler of Sohland, born about 1865, is very probably Alwin’s father. The Sohland birth entry of 1892 will settle it.

Being called Fabrikant in 1926 shows that Alwin already saw himself as a manufacturer, not a shopkeeper. His name had been in the firm name since at least 1921 [1].

The Mattress Factory, 1928 to 1932

FACTBy 1928 the firm is a factory. The Bautzen directory lists “Weikert & Eifler (Inhaber Alwin Eifler), Matratzenfabrik Gnaschwitz (Kontor und Verkaufsstelle)”, with the office and shop at Neusalzaer Straße 38, rear building. The commercial register section reads: “885 Stahldrahtmatratzenfabrik Weickert & Eifler. Inhaber: Alwin Eifler.” Alwin himself is listed as Kaufmann at Neusalzaer Straße 38 [4].

FACTSo by 1928 Alwin was the sole owner (Inhaber). Bruno Weickert is listed that year as a master saddler at Neusalzaer Straße 20, no longer as a partner [4]. When and why he left is UNKNOWNnot yet known; the commercial register file would show it.

FACTThe 1929 and 1932 directories repeat the entries [5][6]. One index of the 1932 book reads No. 36 instead of 38; the two houses are next door to each other.

The Factory in the Spree Valley

FACTThe factory stood in the Spree valley at Gnaschwitz and Singwitz, about 6 km south of Bautzen. The 1928 directory names Gnaschwitz [4]; the later letterhead says the works were on the Industriegelände Singwitz, with a private siding (Anschlußgleis) on the works railway at Gnaschwitz [9].

FACTThat industrial estate had a history of its own. The Royal Saxon powder factory at Gnaschwitz, founded in 1874, was greatly enlarged in the First World War, and in 1916 a works railway was built from Großdöbschütz down the valley to serve it and the Singwitz paper mill. The powder factory closed on 27 December 1919. From 1920 an Interessengemeinschaft Industriegelände Singwitz let its spare buildings to new firms: a fountain pen factory came in 1922 and a farm machinery maker in 1927 [12][13][14]. Weickert & Eifler was one of these newcomers, and the old works railway explains how a small Bautzen firm came to have its own rail siding.

UNKNOWNWhich building the firm used, and whether it still stands, is not yet known. The local history book by Jünger, Die Industriegeschichte des Spreetales zwischen Singwitz und Gnaschwitz (Heimatverein Obergurig, 2011), may name it [12].

The “Elite” Mattress and its Patent

FACTThe letterhead advertises the “Elite” steel wire mattress, “die beste aller Matratzen”, under German Reich patent Nr. 435041, with an elephant standing on a mattress as the trademark. It also lists upholstered toppers (Polsterauflagen), “reform” under-mattresses (Reformunterbetten) and upholstered furniture, and says the firm made its own wire goods and its own wooden frames (eigene Drahtwarenherstellung und Gestelltischlerei) [9].

CORRECTEDThe site used to call the patent the firm’s own. Patent 435041, “Netzmatratze”, belongs to August Filter of Hannover: patented from 12 July 1925 and published 6 October 1926 [11]. Weickert & Eifler made mattresses under Filter’s patent, most likely by licence PROBABLE. This also means the letterhead was printed after October 1926.

Advertising letterhead, Weickert & Eifler, Bautzen. The address Martin-Mutschmann-Straße 19 dates it to 1933 to 1945. Family collection.

Martin-Mutschmann-Straße 19

FACTBy 1935 the firm had moved its office to Martin-Mutschmann-Straße 19, phone 2512: “Weickert & Eifler (Inhaber: A. Eifler)” [7]. CORRECTEDThe site used to say the office was there “by the 1930s”; the records show Neusalzaer Straße 38 until at least 1932 and this address from 1935.

FACTIn 1938 the directory lists Alwin Eifler, Kaufmann, at No. 19, phone 2512, and the house list names the owner of No. 19 as “Eifler, Alwin, Fabrikant”. The commercial register entry is now A 173 [8]. The family’s own copy of a directory page, long thought to be about Alvin Kohlmann, is this 1938 page 14 with Alwin Eifler’s line.

FACTThe street was named after the Nazi governor of Saxony from about 1933 to 1945; its name before and after is Wilthener Straße [18]. The family stayed in the house: Gertrud’s mother Clara Becker died there in 1954 and Gertrud in 1973, both recorded at “Wilthener Straße 19” [15][16].

Bautzen directory 1938, house list: Martin-Mutschmann-Straße 19, owner “Eifler, Alwin, Fabrikant”. SLUB Dresden, public domain.

The War Years

FACTOne business paper survives from the war: an invoice dated 9 April 1943 to Tischlermeister (master carpenter) Erich Lehmann of Klix Nr. 58d, Kreis Bautzen, for one upholstered desk-chair seat at 5,50 Reichsmark plus handling. The firm’s Reichs-Betriebs-Nr. is stamped at the top. At the bottom Gertrud wrote: “Betrag dankend erhalten. Gertrud Eifler. d. 28.4.1943.” [10]

PROBABLEThe invoice shows that Gertrud signed for the firm. The site used to say she ran the books; one signature does not prove that, though it is possible. The letterhead also gives the firm’s banking: Sächsische Bank, Bautzen branch, postal giro Dresden 35383, PO box 30 [9].

Invoice, 9 April 1943, with Gertrud Eifler’s receipt of 28 April 1943. Family collection.

After 1945

UNKNOWNWhat happened to Weickert & Eifler after the war has not been found yet. In Saxony many private firms were expropriated after the referendum of 30 June 1946 [Wikipedia]. Next door on the same industrial estate, the fountain pen maker Gerhard Joh. Quaas was expropriated in 1946 and became VEB Schreibgerätewerk Markant [14]. That is context only; it does not tell us what happened to this firm.

FACTThe family itself stayed in Bautzen. They are at Wilthener Straße 19 in 1954 and 1973 [15][16].

PROBABLEThe family chart says Alwin died on 18 December 1968 of a skull fracture after a car accident. His death is not in the Bautzen town death index or register for 1968 [17], so it was probably registered where the accident happened, in a village outside the town. Gertrud is recorded as a widow in 1973 [16].

Everyone Involved

Ernst Alwin Eifler (1892 to 1968). Partner by 1921, sole owner from at least 1928 to at least 1943. Born in Sohland an der Spree. Christa Kohlmann’s father. FACT[1][3][4][8]

Bruno Weickert. Master saddler, the “Weickert” of the firm. Ran the saddlery and upholstery shop at Löbauer Straße 10 in 1921, living at Neusalzaer Straße 34; at Neusalzaer Straße 20 in 1928, no longer named in the firm. Dates not yet known. FACT[1][4]

Karl Gottlieb Weickert. Master shoemaker, Neusalzaer Straße 20, 1917 and 1921. Probably Bruno’s father. LEAD[1][2]

Johanne Weickert, widow, at Neusalzaer Straße 20 in 1928. Perhaps Karl’s widow. LEAD[4]

Gertrud Elise Eifler, née Becker (1905 to 1973). Alwin’s wife; signed for the firm in 1943. FACT[3][10][16]

Ernst Eifler (born about 1865). Farmer at Sohland an der Spree Nr. 247; witness at Alwin’s wedding. Probably Alwin’s father. LEAD[3]

Richard Hermann Becker (1875 to 1938). Gertrud’s father, an ironmonger in Dresden; the second witness at the wedding. FACT[3]

Erich Lehmann. Master carpenter at Klix, a customer in 1943. FACT[10]

August Filter of Hannover. Holder of the “Netzmatratze” patent 435041 used on the Elite mattress. Not connected to the family. FACT[11]

UNKNOWNNo employees’ names have been found yet. The commercial register file and any post-1945 files would name managers, Prokuristen or a trustee.
